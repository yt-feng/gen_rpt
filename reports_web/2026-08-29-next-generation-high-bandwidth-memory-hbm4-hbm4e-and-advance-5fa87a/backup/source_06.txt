Samsung Electronics Co. ( SSNLF , Financials ), the South Korean memory-chip giant , has reportedly pushed its HBM4 production yield to about 80%, reaching a target it originally expected to hit only by the end of the year.

That is a meaningful improvement from below 60% when mass production began in February.
Better yields mean Samsung can produce more usable chips from the same manufacturing capacity, potentially lowering costs and making it easier to ramp shipments as demand for AI memory keeps climbing.
The company reportedly plans to more than triple HBM4 revenue in the third quarter, with the newer memory expected to account for more than 60% of its HBM sales during the second half.
Samsung is also targeting roughly 38% of the HBM market by year-end. For investors, the bigger read-through is competition.
More Samsung supply could give Nvidia additional options for its upcoming Vera Rubin AI systems while increasing pressure on SK Hynix and Micron. The next test is whether Samsung can turn its improving yields into meaningful market-share gains.
