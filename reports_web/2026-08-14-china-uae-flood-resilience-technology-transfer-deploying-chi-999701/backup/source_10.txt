On April 16, 2024, Al Ain City in the United Arab Emirates experienced a record-breaking flash flood, with over 250 mm of rainfall in a few hours among the most extreme hydroclimatic events recorded in the region. This study presents a detailed geospatial assessment of the event, integrating optical satellite imagery, climatic data, and topographic analysis to quantify flood extent, depth, and impact. Sentinel-2 imagery acquired 12 h after peak rainfall enabled high-resolution flood mapping. To improve floodwater detection, we applied the newly developed Index of Turbid Waters (ITW), a spectral index designed to distinguish turbid floodwaters from permanent water bodies. The integration of ITW with rainfall data from 16 meteorological stations and digital elevation models revealed a strong relationship between flood extent and geomorphological vulnerability. Water depth estimations identified critical inundation zones, with depths exceeding 8 m in urban districts such as Rowdah. GIS-based infrastructure and land use analysis highlighted high-risk areas, including transportation networks and informal flood defenses. The findings underscore the increasing risk of extreme flash floods in arid environments and demonstrate how rapid, multi-source geospatial analysis can inform emergency response and urban resilience planning.
Around the world, the number of natural disasters and weather-related events like storms or floods has increased significantly by 3 times over the past 35 years 1 . From an economic standpoint, globally socio-economic issues like an increase in population as well as changes in demographics are the strongest drivers for increasing loss; benefit - cost analysis suggests that global warming is a contributing factor due to convective phenomena such as hailstorms coupled with precipitation which show the highest grieving debts from people living in areas more prone to seasonal calamities. The insurance industry is heavily impacted by these trends, compelling changes in pricing strategy and risk evaluation to keep policies solvent 1 .
Integrating remotely sensed and climatic data is crucial for detecting and analyzing flooded zones in arid regions. These areas are highly vulnerable to flash floods due to their low vegetation cover, limited soil permeability, and unpredictable rainfall patterns. By combining satellite imagery with precipitation data, it is possible to monitor rainfall events, analyze surface runoff, and map inundated areas with high accuracy 2 , 3 , 4 . This combined approach enables real-time flood detection and enhances the precision of hydrological models, which represent an important component of early warning systems. Additionally, it aids in identifying flood-prone zones, allowing policymakers and local governments to implement appropriate mitigation strategies, optimize water resource management, and minimize the socioeconomic and environmental impacts of floods. Such integrated approaches provide key insights into the dynamics of flooding in arid regions, helping communities improve their preparedness for this natural hazard.
Many studies worldwide have investigated the combination of remotely sensed data with climatic data to characterize natural flooding hazards and mitigate them. Brivio et al. 5 developed a method combining synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images and geographic information systems (GIS) data to accurately map flood areas, thereby overcoming the delay between peak flood inundation and data acquisition and successfully mapping 96.7% of the flood area recorded by local authorities during the 1994 flooding in Regione Piemonte, Italy 5 .
Studies of floodplain hydrodynamics, such as in the Biebrza River (Poland), show that floods originated from spatially distinct water sources (e.g., rivers, groundwater, and rainwater) and are influenced by vegetation patterns, with methodologies such as remote sensing and hydrochemical analyses able to effectively map inundation and water distribution 6 . Crétaux et al. 7 discuss the use of remote sensing data for flood mapping, highlighting the effectiveness of satellite imagery in detecting the extent of flooding and assessing flood risks 7 . They apply various remote sensing techniques, emphasizing their application in monitoring and managing flood events.
Abou Samra 8 used remote sensing, GIS, and cartographic modeling to assess the impacts of sea level rise on Port Said, Egypt, revealing that projected coastal flooding under 1.0 to 3.0 m sea level rise scenarios could severely affect land use and coastal features, emphasizing the need for proactive planning and mitigation. Kocaman et al. 9 demonstrated the effectiveness of applying machine learning techniques to multisensor remote sensing data to assess flood extent and landslide susceptibility following a catastrophic meteorological event in Ordu, Turkey, highlighting the importance of advanced Earth observation technologies for disaster monitoring and mitigation.
Tavus et al. 10 assessed flood mapping accuracy for the 2020 Sardoba Reservoir dam failure using random forest classification based on Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 optical data. Their study found that fusing both datasets significantly improves the detection of flooded areas, including inundated vegetation, thus highlighting the value of combining pre-event optical data with SAR data in the absence of post-event optical imagery. Tripathy and Malladi 11 introduced the Global Flood Mapper, a user-friendly web application that leverages Sentinel-1 data and improved flood mapping techniques—including peak flood detection and terrain-based false positive reduction—to provide timely, accurate flood extent maps, thereby supporting emergency response and disaster management efforts worldwide.
Yu et al. 12 developed a multi-dimensional flood risk assessment framework combining hazard, exposure, and vulnerability indices with advanced weighting and clustering methods to map and analyze flood risk distribution in China’s Han River Basin, highlighting high-risk areas primarily located in the basin’s mid–lower reaches and the mitigating effects of reservoir regulation. Portalés‑Julià et al. 13 presented a cloud-aware machine learning framework for accurate and rapid satellite-based flood mapping using Sentinel-2 and Landsat data, which demonstrated improved performance over traditional methods and was able to successfully map major flood events in Pakistan and Australia in 2022 to support emergency response and damage assessment.
Petropoulos et al. 14 presented a threshold-based method using Sentinel-2 data and spectral water indices (normalized difference water index (NDWI) and modified NDWI) to map the extent of flooding in Northern Italy to support disaster management and post-flood recovery efforts. Their study demonstrated that both indices were able to effectively detect inundated areas, with NDWI exhibiting slightly higher accuracy.
Abou Samra et al. 15 used multi-temporal Sentinel-1 SAR imagery and Google Earth Engine to map the extensive flooding and environmental impacts of the 2023 Kakhovka Dam breach in Ukraine, revealing significant inundation and highlighting the event’s adverse effects on land use and soil organic carbon sequestration, with implications for post-disaster restoration and management. Amatebelle et al. 16 analyzed remote sensing and GIS applications in flood disaster risk management over the past two decades worldwide, highlighting the growing role of these technologies in flood assessment and prediction, regional research disparities (especially in Africa), and recommending the enhanced use of these technologies to improve global flood resilience.
Albertini et al. 17 evaluated the effectiveness of two random forest models using Sentinel-2 imagery and multispectral indices for accurately mapping flood extents, demonstrating the strong performance of these models in detecting inundated areas during a flooding event in Italy in 2020.
