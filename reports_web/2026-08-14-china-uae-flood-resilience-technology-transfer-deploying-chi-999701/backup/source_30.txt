Climate and temperatures in the United Arab Emirates

Temperature records of the last 43 years
Winters are generally mild and pleasant, with average temperatures between 15 °C and 25 °C. Inland, nights are considerably cooler during this time of year. There is little rainfall in most parts of the Arab Emirates. The annual rainfall is less than 100 mm and the summers from May to October are practically rain-free. Only from December to March it rains occasionally. The coldest day in these 43 years was reported by the Abu Dhabi weather station. Here the temperature dropped to 9.3 °C in January 2021. Abu Dhabi lies at an altitude of 27 meters above sea level. The coldest winter (December to February) was in 1983 with an average temperature of 18.6 °C. In the United Arab Emirates, it is usual to have about 2.7 degrees more at 21.3 °C for this three-month period. The most precipitation fell in February 1988. With 5.4 mm per day, the Abu Dhabi Bateen weather station recorded the highest monthly average of the last 43 years. Data basis and methodology The data from the individual measuring stations are based on the archives of the German Weather Service, individual values averaged and supplemented by own elements. In order to determine a representative national average, average values were first calculated for each part of the country, which were then summarized at the national level. Thus, if a disproportionate number of weather stations are located in a small area, their number does not affect the national average. There are 3 stations in the United Arab Emirates itself. In 4 cases, neighboring but nearby weather stations were also used to obtain more accurate values. Difference between climate and weather The weather is a snapshot at a specific point in time or over a short period of time. This page deals with the climate on the basis of average values over a long period of time - in our case the past 20 years. In addition, an entire country is covered here on the basis of 7 weather stations, representing not only the major cities but also remote areas at various elevations.
