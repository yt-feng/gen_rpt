The accuracy and reliability of landslide displacement predictions are critical to disaster early warning systems; however, most current research focuses on improving numerical precision while overlooking the potentially severe consequences of displacement underestimation. This paper proposes a landslide displacement prediction method that integrates a risk-aversion mechanism. In this approach, double exponential smoothing is employed to capture long-term trend changes in landslide displacement, and a hybrid model combining Support Vector Regression (SVR) and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBOOST) is developed to characterize periodic displacement fluctuations. To address the risk of underestimation, a loss function incorporating a penalty factor is designed, and a constraint mechanism is introduced to adjust periodic displacement predictions toward a higher confidence interval. The predicted trend and periodic displacement components are then superimposed to generate a complete cumulative displacement forecast. In an empirical evaluation using the Dayangshan landslide in Suzhou, the proposed risk-averse prediction method not only maintains high predictive accuracy but also significantly reduces the rate of displacement underestimation, enabling dynamic and adaptive optimization of early warning strategies and risk control.
Landslide disasters occur with high frequency, exhibit dense spatial distribution, affect wide areas, and cause severe damage ( Liu X. et al., 2025 ; KC et al., 2024 ; Titus et al., 2023 ). Slope instability not only leads to ecological degradation but also results in substantial economic losses ( Cukur et al., 2025 ). Accurately predicting horizontal displacement changes in landslides can effectively mitigate disaster risks and has emerged as a cost-effective and practical strategy for disaster prevention and mitigation ( Liu H. et al., 2025 ; Wang Y. F. et al., 2025 ).
The horizontal displacement of landslides is highly complex, and its prediction remains a core challenge in natural hazard research ( Duan et al., 2023 ; Achu et al., 2025 ). As a typical nonlinear dynamical system, landslide movement is influenced by multiple coupled factors, including geological structure, topography, and human engineering activities ( Alcántara-Ayala, 2025 ). At present, research on landslide displacement prediction primarily emphasizes the quantitative assessment of point prediction accuracy, yet generally neglects the stochastic variability of prediction outcomes and its impact on overall system risk ( Wang et al., 2022 ). Traditional evaluation systems usually use indicators such as root mean square error or mean absolute error to measure model performance, but such methods cannot reflect the fluctuation characteristics of the predicted value within the confidence interval, and the risk loss is not included in the optimization objective ( Maghfuri and Kuku, 2025 ). For instance, in a landslide displacement prediction scenario, if a model outputs a displacement value of 0.9 m while the actual monitored value is 1.0 m, the absolute error is only 0.1 m. Nevertheless, such underestimation may mislead decision-makers into perceiving the landslide as stable, potentially delaying necessary engineering interventions and ultimately leading to sudden instability under critical conditions. The core issue underlying such underestimation events is that traditional accuracy metrics reflect only numerical discrepancies between predicted and observed values, without quantifying how prediction uncertainty propagates into landslide risk decision-making ( Parvathareddy et al., 2025 ).
The essence of a risk-aversion strategy lies in reducing the likelihood of risk occurrence or mitigating potential losses through proactive intervention ( Huff et al., 2022 ). In the context of landslide displacement prediction, risk aversion does not simply imply conservative predictions; rather, it involves introducing probabilistic forecasting frameworks—such as Bayesian neural networks or quantile regression—to transform displacement predictions into probability distribution estimates. These estimates can then be combined with risk matrix methods to evaluate the probability of landslide instability under different forecast confidence levels ( Li Y. et al., 2021 ). Fundamentally, this risk-based decision-making paradigm shifts the approach from “post-event remediation” to “pre-event control” through proactive intervention, thereby avoiding the “catastrophic underestimation trap” that traditional precision-oriented methods may encounter ( Ronco et al., 2023 ). Recently, the concept of risk aversion has generated considerable research interest, akin to a stone cast into the ocean of academic and applied inquiry ( Halter et al., 2025 ). Numerous researchers and industry experts have explored effective risk-aversion pathways and strategies ( Pugliese et al., 2021 ). As research has deepened, risk aversion has successfully crossed disciplinary boundaries and found wide application in structural prediction across various fields. Whether in the financial sector’s efforts to hedge against market volatility and forecast economic structures, or in engineering construction’s attempts to evade potential risks and predict structural stability, risk aversion plays a vital role in providing more reliable safeguards for development across domains ( Allioui and Mourdi, 2023 ; Okoroafor et al., 2022 ; Chen et al., 2024 ). In recent years, time series prediction methods based on signal decomposition (such as VMD) and state space models (such as Mamba) have demonstrated excellent performance in complex system modeling, providing a new research perspective for landslide displacement prediction ( Zhu et al., 2025 ; Wang C. et al., 2025 ; Ma et al., 2025 ; Guo et al., 2026 ).
Currently, research on risk aversion in landslide displacement forecasting is evolving along two main trajectories. The first trajectory centers on probabilistic forecasting and uncertainty quantification, employing methods such as Bayesian frameworks, quantile regression, and Conformal Prediction. These approaches characterize uncertainty by outputting prediction intervals or full probability distributions, thereby providing standardized theoretical tools for decision-making based on risk matrices ( Li F. et al., 2021 ). The second trajectory focuses on improving the accuracy of deterministic point forecasts, using loss functions like mean squared error that typically penalize overestimation and underestimation equally, without integrating specific risk-aversion preferences into the model optimization objectives. Consequently, within the paradigm of deterministic point forecasting, research remains insufficient on how to proactively and directly mitigate specific risks arising from systematic underestimation through redesigning loss functions. The methodological framework in this regard requires further enrichment and development. The aim of this paper is to address this gap by proposing a novel deterministic point forecasting method that incorporates a risk-aversion mechanism. In addition, the data driven technique as a new research method, while in other areas show the excellent performance, but in the complex landslide risk assessment system, its application is still in the exploratory stage ( Bao et al., 2022 ). The purpose of this paper is to reduce the underestimation of the prediction results caused by data uncertainty and model limitations in the process of landslide displacement prediction. Therefore, this paper proposes to construct a hybrid model based on support vector machine and extreme gradient boosting algorithm, which integrates the advantages of the two models to improve the prediction accuracy and avoid risks. Taking the Dayangshan landslide in Suzhou as a specific case, the application effect of the hybrid model method in the actual landslide displacement prediction and risk assessment is empirically analyzed and verified.
Double exponential smoothing (DES) is a method that applies exponential smoothing a second time to the initially smoothed values ( Kahraman and Akay, 2023 ; Rabbani et al., 2021 ). It cannot be used independently for prediction; rather, it must be combined with single exponential smoothing to establish a mathematical model for forecasting. For initial value determination, when observations exhibit a stable trend, the first data point can generally be used directly as the initial value. If the change trend of the observed value fluctuates, the average value of n data should be used as the initial value to reduce the impact of the initial value on the smooth value. The second exponential smoothing method essentially takes the weighted average of the historical data as the forecast result of the future time. It has the advantages of simple calculation, less sample requirement, stronger adaptability and more stable results. For this study, Support Vector Regression (SVR) and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBOOST) were selected as the base models for constructing a hybrid predictor. This choice is primarily motivated by the following three considerations, which align closely with the objectives and constraints of this research.
The primary contribution of this paper lies in proposing and validating a general risk-aversion-embedded framework for landslide displacement forecasting (including temporal decomposition, hybrid architecture, and asymmetric loss functions), rather than conducting a comparative analysis of different base forecasting model architectures. As classic and powerful machine learning models, SVR and XGBOOST have been extensively validated for their effectiveness and robustness across various nonlinear regression tasks. They provide a reliable and reproducible baseline performance for verifying the proposed framework please see the Figure 1 .
The acquisition of landslide monitoring data is costly, and long-term, high-frequency, high-quality samples are often limited. While deep learning models (such as LSTM, GRU, or Transformer) possess strong representational capabilities, their full performance potential typically depends on large-scale training data. With limited samples, they are prone to overfitting, and their training processes are computationally complex. In contrast, SVR and XGBOOST generally demonstrate better sample efficiency and robustness on small-to-medium-sized datasets, with faster training and inference speeds. This aligns more closely with the computational efficiency and deployment practicality requirements of real-world monitoring systems.
Compared to complex deep learning “black boxes,” SVR and XGBOOST offer relatively better model interpretability (e.g., feature importance analysis in XGBOOST), helping engineering personnel understand model decisions and enhancing the method’s credibility and practicality. Furthermore, it is particularly emphasized that the proposed risk-aversion framework is model-agnostic. Its core concepts (decomposition, hybridization, asymmetric loss) can naturally be integrated with any modern forecasting model, such as LSTM or Transformer. This work uses classic models to validate the framework’s effectiveness, laying a solid foundation for future integration and optimization of more advanced deep learning architectures within this framework.
SVR and XGBOOST are both well-established prediction models in landslide displacement forecasting ( Sahin, 2023 ). SVR is developed from the Support Vector Machine (SVM). The basic idea of SVR is to map training data from the input space to a high-dimensional feature space via a kernel function and then construct a separating hyperplane with maximum margin in that feature space. The goal of regression problems is to determine a function that accurately estimates future values ( Han et al., 2021 ). XGBOOST (Extreme Gradient Boosting) is an efficient gradient boosting decision tree algorithm widely used in machine learning tasks on structured data ( Shi et al., 2022 ). XGBOOST significantly improves prediction performance by integrating multiple weak learners (decision trees) and gradually optimizing the model through gradient boosting. The core idea is to iteratively train a series of tree models, with each tree committed to correcting the prediction errors of the previous tree, ultimately obtaining a strong prediction model through weighted summation ( Xu et al., 2022 ). Therefore, this paper develops a hybrid model, which can maintain the positive part of each modeling method and prevent the problem of diminishing marginal utility of simple prediction model. To clarify the positioning of the method proposed in this paper, it is essential to articulate its fundamental distinction from existing risk-aversion approaches, such as Bayesian or quantile regression methods. The latter inherently belong to the “probabilistic forecasting” paradigm, whose core lies in quantifying uncertainty (by outputting prediction intervals or distributions), after which decision-makers must incorporate external risk models for subsequent analysis. In contrast, the method proposed herein falls under the “risk-adjusted point forecasting” paradigm. The key differences are as follows.
Traditional point forecasts minimize symmetric loss functions, whereas this study designs an asymmetric penalizing loss function ( Equation 6 ) that internalizes the risk preference of “reducing underestimation” directly into the optimization objective during model training.
Probabilistic methods output information (distributions/intervals) that describe uncertainty, leading to a relatively indirect decision-making pathway. The method proposed here directly outputs a single, safety-adjusted forecast value—shifted toward the conservative side (i.e., less prone to underestimation). This results in a more direct decision-making pathway, aiming to provide a reference point with a built-in safety margin for binary warning decisions, such as whether to trigger an alarm.
Probabilistic methods excel at comprehensively characterizing uncertainty but may demand more data and computational resources. The approach presented here offers a lightweight, interpretable solution for achieving risk aversion in scenarios that prioritize decision efficiency, operate under resource constraints, or favor the use of point forecasts. The two paradigms emphasize different aspects and complement each other.
