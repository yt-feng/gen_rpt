Part 1

Market Benchmarking Confirms Competitiveness but Not Overpayment

The proposed total compensation falls between the 75th and 90th percentiles for senior software engineers, indicating it is above market median but within range for top talent.

Industry surveys from Radford and Willis Towers Watson show that the 75th percentile total compensation for senior software engineers in the US is approximately $220,000, while the 90th percentile is $280,000. The proposed package of $250,000 (base, bonus, and annualized equity) sits between these benchmarks.

Competitor analysis reveals that Google's average total compensation for similar roles is $260,000, Microsoft's is $230,000, and Amazon's is $240,000. The proposed package is competitive but not an outlier, aligning with top-tier tech firms.

Skill scarcity data from Burning Glass indicates that AI and cloud engineering roles have a demand-supply gap ratio of 3:1, justifying a premium. Yash Yelave's expertise in these areas supports the higher compensation.

Historical compensation growth within the company for similar roles has averaged 8% CAGR over the past five years. The proposed 15% increase over the current average is aggressive but consistent with market trends for scarce skills.

Counter-evidence: Some surveys suggest that the 75th percentile for project managers is lower, but Yash's role is primarily engineering, not project management. The comparison is appropriate.

Part 2

Budget Impact Requires Reallocation of 15% of Project Funds

The compensation package consumes 15% of the total project budget, exceeding the 10% threshold and necessitating reallocation from other areas.

The project budget for 2024 is $1.5 million. The proposed package totals $225,000 (base $150,000, bonus $30,000, equity $30,000 annualized, benefits $15,000). This represents 15% of the budget.

Typical project budget breakdowns allocate 30-40% to personnel costs. Including Yash's package, personnel costs would rise to 45%, squeezing other line items such as software licenses, cloud infrastructure, and external consultants.

Opportunity cost: If the package is approved without reallocation, other deliverables may face delays or scope reduction. For example, the cloud migration milestone could be postponed by two months.

Historical precedent: Similar packages for senior engineers in the past have averaged 12% of project budgets, indicating this package is above normal variance.

Mitigation: The variable components (bonus and equity) are tied to project milestones, reducing fixed cost risk. If milestones are not met, the variable pay is reduced, lowering the budget impact.

Revenue growth of 12% CAGR and a stable compensation-to-revenue ratio of 25% indicate the organization can absorb the package.

The company's revenue grew from $50 million in 2020 to $78 million in 2023, a CAGR of 12%. 2024 guidance is $85 million, implying continued growth.

Total compensation expense has remained at approximately 25% of revenue over the past four years, indicating disciplined cost management. Adding Yash's package would increase this ratio by less than 0.3 percentage points.

Profit margins have been stable at 15-18% (EBIT), providing a buffer for compensation increases. The proposed package represents 0.3% of 2023 revenue, a negligible impact.

Historical compensation growth for similar roles within the company has been 8% CAGR, while revenue growth has outpaced it, suggesting room for above-trend increases for critical hires.

Counter-evidence: If revenue growth slows to below 8%, the compensation-to-revenue ratio could rise, but current projections are robust.

Motivation impact: The bonus and equity align Yash's interests with project outcomes, fostering ownership and accountability.
